Grandma Plush Blueprint

A soft toy that listens while its paw is squeezed and answers in grandma's cloned voice. A projector home base that shows the animals she talks about. The cloud software that joins them. This page lists every part, how each one is set up, what we build, and where hardware meets software.

Part AHardware, owned by the hardware team Part BSoftware, built with Claude Code Verify means not yet confirmed on real hardware or in official docs

The toy

ESP32-S3 board, I2S microphone and amplifier, 3 W speaker, paw button, LED eyes and a USB power bank. Squeeze the paw to talk, let go to hear grandma.

≈ ₹3,500–4,000 per toy

The home base

A Portronics Beem 440 projector inside a small house. It runs one web page full-screen and plays an animal clip on the wall when the cloud gives the cue.

≈ ₹6,799 + kiosk licence

The cloud

A relay on Fly.io joins Scribe (hearing), Claude Haiku 5.5 (thinking) and Eleven v4 Turbo (grandma's voice). Supabase keeps the memory and pushes live events.

Target: first word ≈ 1 s after release

00

How it fits together

The toy talks to one thing only: our relay. The relay calls the three AI services and writes everything to Supabase. The projector and the parent website listen to Supabase. Neither of them ever talks to the toy directly, so each piece can fail without taking the others down.

H2

Where each part sits

  • Microphone in the face, sound hole facing out behind a small opening, at least 10 cm from the speaker, with foam behind it so stuffing cannot rub the port.
  • Speaker in the tummy, cone 5–10 mm behind the outer fabric. Only thin fabric or felt in front. If the fleece is thick, cut a 30–40 mm hole behind a felt patch.
  • Board and power bank in the zipped pouch, the power bank's USB port facing the zip.
  • Paw button inside a rigid disc (a bottle cap or a printed puck) so a squeeze anywhere on the paw presses it. Run the cable up the arm with slack.
  • LED eyes behind the existing eye buttons, frosted cabochons, or a felt heart on the chest.

H4

Assembly, in order

  1. Bench first. Wire everything on a breadboard, flash the test firmware, and check mic, speaker and button. Solder only after that works.
  2. Perfboard. Solder onto perfboard with female headers so the ESP32 can be pulled out and swapped.
  3. Tray. Fix the perfboard inside the ABS box and cut a slot for the USB-C lead. Glue the speaker to its own small baffle facing outward, so it pushes air through the fabric and not into stuffing.
  4. Strain relief. Anchor every wire where it leaves the box with a cable tie or hot glue. Squeezed paws are the most common cause of broken wires.
  5. Open the toy. Unpick 10–12 cm of the back seam, or use the zip. Remove stuffing from the torso to make a cavity.
  6. Pouch. Sew in a cotton or felt pouch with a zip or Velcro flap. The box and power bank go inside.
  7. Place the parts as in H2: speaker in the tummy, mic in the face, button puck in the paw, LEDs at the eyes.
  8. Re-stuff around the speaker and mic, never in front of them.
  9. Shake test. Close the toy and shake it while it talks. Add foam or glue wherever something buzzes or rattles.
  10. Final test. Zipped, on battery, in a noisy room, for 30 minutes. Then feel the toy for warmth.
H5

Sound, power and safety

Sound

  • Half-duplex push-to-talk. The mic is off while grandma speaks, so the toy never hears itself and needs no echo cancellation.
  • Capture 16 kHz mono.
  • Mic level. The INMP441 sends 24-bit samples in a 32-bit slot. Shift right by 11–14 bits, not 16, so a soft child's voice stays loud enough. Test with a real 5–8-year-old at 30–50 cm.
  • Output. Amp gain 12 dB, software volume around 70–80 %, so speech stays clean through fabric in a loud room.
  • Rattles come from loose wires touching the speaker cone and from the box resonating. Foam-tape everything.

Power and safety

  • Power bank, not a bare LiPo. It has its own protected cell, charger and 5 V regulator.
  • Leave it idle for 30 minutes to check it does not switch itself off at low current.
  • Budget. Board and mic draw about 120–200 mA while streaming, with amp bursts of a few hundred mA. A 5,000–10,000 mAh bank covers a full day. These figures are calculated from the Espressif datasheet; measure them.
  • Leave an air gap in the box and never wrap the board in foam. It runs warm, not hot.
  • No loose small parts: sew LED covers in place. Zip on the back, never the face. Charge outside the toy.
H6

The projector home base

The home base is a small decorated house with a projector inside. The projector shows one web page full-screen all the time. When grandma mentions an animal, the page plays that animal's clip, with sound, on the wall. It is mains-powered and independent of the toy.

Projector

Portronics Beem 440 (POR 2106): Android 11, 720p native, 3 W speaker, rotatable stand, 850 g, ≈ ₹6,799 on Amazon.in.

It is rated at "1800 lumens", which is not an ANSI figure. Treat it as a dim-room projector: a 60–80 cm image from 1–1.5 m away. Verify

Kiosk app

Fully Kiosk Browser with the Plus licence (€8.90 or US$10.99 plus tax, one-time, per device). Sideload the APK if the Play Store is missing.

It locks the projector to our page and keeps it awake.

The house

  • Cardboard or MDF, painted, with a window for the lens and a vent for the fan.
  • The projector sits on its rotatable stand inside; the toy sits on the porch or beside it.
  • Extension board and HDMI cable stored in the house for the fallback.

  • Spare projector: Magcubic HY300 Pro, ≈ ₹5,098, only if the delivery date works. Android version varies by batch.
H7

Network kit

  • Our own 4G/5G hotspot, set to 2.4 GHz, WPA2, with a simple network name. The ESP32-S3 has no 5 GHz radio, and public Wi-Fi often has login pages the toy cannot pass.
  • A second phone on another operator as backup. Both network names and passwords are stored in the toy.
  • The toy and the projector both join the hotspot. Wi-Fi power-save is turned off in the toy's firmware.

S2

Toy firmware

Starting point

ElatoAI firmware-arduino, PlatformIO environment esp32-s3-devkitc-1. It already does I2S microphone capture, Opus encode and decode, a secure WebSocket, a button, an RGB LED, a Wi-Fi setup page and factory reset.

First wiring test

Before ElatoAI, flash a ready-made xiaozhi-esp32 breadboard firmware from its releases page. It confirms the mic and speaker wiring in minutes. Then switch to the ElatoAI fork.

What we change

  • Point it at our relay's wss:// address with a per-toy token. No AI keys on the toy, ever.
  • Send ptt_start on press and ptt_end on release, with 20–30 ms debounce.
  • Open the connection at power-on and keep it open with pings. A cold connect takes 3–4 seconds, too slow to do on each press.
  • 16 kHz mono capture, Opus in 20 ms frames, Wi-Fi power-save off.
  • Playback buffer of 80–150 ms; larger only if audio stutters.
  • Store 5–6 short grandma sounds ("Hmm!", "Achha!") and one "Grandma is having a nap, press my paw again" clip in flash. Play a "Hmm!" the instant the paw is released; play the nap clip on network errors.
  • If the paw is pressed while grandma is talking, stop playback at once on the toy and send ptt_start.
  • Time release-to-first-sound on the toy's own clock and send it to the relay each turn.
  • Store two Wi-Fi networks; reconnect with back-off from 250 ms to 4 s.
  • Audio rate: ElatoAI plays 24 kHz by default. Either keep 24 kHz end to end or change the playback rate to 16 kHz. Resample in one place only.

S4

One turn, start to finish

  1. The child presses the paw. The eyes turn green, the toy sends ptt_start and starts streaming.
  2. The relay opens a Scribe stream loaded with this round's hint words and forwards the audio.
  3. The child lets go. The toy plays a stored "Hmm!" at once and sends ptt_end.
  4. The relay commits the Scribe stream and gets the final text.
  5. The safety word list and the game referee run, in a few milliseconds.
  6. Claude Haiku 5.5 starts grandma's reply from the cached persona and session brief.
  7. Each finished sentence goes straight to Eleven v4 Turbo and is flushed, so speech starts before the reply is complete.
  8. Audio streams back. The toy buffers 80–150 ms and plays it; the eyes turn purple.
  9. If Claude called show_animal, the projector gets its cue as that sentence starts playing.
  10. Each step's timing is written to Supabase; the toy reports release-to-first-sound from its own clock.
S5

Inside the relay

Hearing

  • Scribe realtime over WebSocket, commit_strategy: manual, committed on ptt_end.
  • A fresh stream on each press, loaded with that round's hint words: at most 50, each up to 20 characters.
  • Language set to en, never auto-detect: short child utterances get misdetected.

Before Claude

  • Safety word list in English, Hindi and Hinglish. A hit skips Claude and plays a gentle pre-made reply.
  • Game referee: first letter, category, and close matches such as "jiraf" for giraffe. The verdict goes into Claude's turn, so Claude never has to judge.

Claude

  • Anthropic's own Messages API. Its OpenAI-compatible endpoint drops prompt caching.
  • claude-haiku-5-5, thinking off or effort low Verify
  • Two cached blocks: grandma's persona, then the session brief. Warm the cache when a session starts.
  • About 150 tokens, 1–2 sentences. Tools: show_animal, alert_parent.

Voice

  • wss://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/text-to-dialogue/stream-input?model_id=eleven_v4_turbo&output_format=pcm_16000
  • First message registers grandma's voice id, stability 0.5.
  • Flush after every sentence: first audio in about 140 ms instead of about 355 ms.
  • keep_alive every 15 s, because the socket closes after 20 s idle. To cut grandma off, close and reopen it (about 90 ms).
  • v4 Turbo is refused on the older text-to-speech WebSocket. Use text-to-dialogue.

"Roar like a lion"

  • Skip speech-to-text for imitation turns.
  • Measure loudness on 20 ms frames. At least 300 ms above the noise floor plus 12 dB counts as a roar.
  • Play a pre-made reaction at once, then tell Claude "the child roared for 1.4 s, loudly".

Guards and hosting

  • Fixed game lines, praise and the "Hmm!" sounds are pre-made with eleven_v4 and cached.
  • No first word after 1.2 s: play "Let me think…". After 4 s: a safe pre-made line and move on.
  • Fly.io has no connection time limit; Vercel functions close WebSockets at their maximum duration.
  • Pick the region by pinging api.elevenlabs.io and api.anthropic.com from Mumbai, Singapore and US-East. Default US-East.
S6

The projector page

  • Page: a Next.js route on Vercel, /home-base/[toyId], opened full-screen by Fully Kiosk.
  • On load: fetch the clip list, preload every MP4, then subscribe to the Supabase Realtime channel room:<toyId>.
  • Cue: show_animal {name, delay_ms}. The relay broadcasts it when the sentence that names the animal starts going to the toy. The page waits delay_ms, about 250 ms (toy buffer plus downlink), then plays, so the picture lands with grandma's words.
  • Idle: a calm scene. When a clip ends, the page returns to it.
  • Speed meter: an optional corner number fed by metrics:<toyId>, showing release-to-first-word for each turn.
  • Blocked sound: play the clip muted and let the toy make the animal sound.

The projector never talks to the toy. If the projector is off, the game still works with sound only.

S7

Where the toy meets memory

  • Session start: the relay loads the latest session brief from Supabase: who the child is, today's plan, the parents' guidance and the hint words.
  • Every turn: the relay writes the turn, the game verdict, safety flags and each step's timing, plus an agent event for the live screens.
  • Guidance changes mid-session: the Planner writes a new brief; the relay swaps it in at the next turn and grandma says a one-line confirmation.
  • Session end: the relay marks the session ended. That queues the Memory Keeper, then the Planner writes tomorrow's brief.

E

Check before relying on it

  • Our pin map against ElatoAI's default pin settings.
  • Claude Haiku 5.5 with thinking off: the real time to its first word from our relay.
  • v4 Turbo on the text-to-dialogue WebSocket. The official reference still says v3 only; a third-party test says v4 Turbo works.
  • Whether Scribe v3 Live supports manual commit; if not, use Scribe v2 Realtime.
  • Grandma's clone shows as ready for v4.
  • Beem 440: real brightness, Play Store access, and unmuted autoplay inside Fully Kiosk.
  • The chosen power bank does not switch itself off at low current.
  • Every price marked as an estimate, and delivery dates before ordering.
